section: Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio
  sub: Last 7 days of training load vs the last 28 — minutes in each heart-rate zone weighted by intensity, a linear load like the ones these bands were built on. A widely used injury-risk measure, though later research disputes how well the bands predict injury.
section: Training variety
  sub: Your strain each day, then how evenly the week's load was spread, then what you actually did. Training at the same effort every day has been linked to more illness than the same total with lighter days in between.
section: Time in heart-rate zones
  sub: Easy is below about 82% of your max heart rate, moderate 82–87%, hard above 87% — the three zones used in endurance research, converted from WHOOP's zones with your own resting and max heart rate. Strength sessions are left out, since most of their time is rest between sets. The polarization index (Treff et al. 2019) sums the mix in one number: above 2.00 it is polarized — mostly easy, some hard, little in between. It describes your mix; studies disagree on whether polarized training beats other mixes.
section: Sessions per week
  sub: Activity minutes count moderate time once and vigorous time twice, as the WHO guideline does: 150+ a week (WHO 2020). Vigorous is 60%+ of your heart-rate reserve (ACSM), which is WHOOP's zone 2 and up; moderate is zone 1. Only logged workouts count, strength sessions aside, and the easiest moderate minutes sit inside WHOOP's zone 0, so this can only come out low.
section: Recovery cost by sport
  sub: How your recovery the next morning compares after each sport, against your other training days. A ~ means the gap is approximate — it could still be chance.

section: Body clock
  sub: Social jetlag (Wittmann et al. 2006) is how far the middle of your sleep moves between work nights and weekend nights — like flying across time zones every weekend. Natural mid-sleep is the weekend middle corrected for catch-up sleep, the standard measure of chronotype (Roenneberg et al. 2012): earlier means more of a morning type. Weekend nights are the ones ending on Saturday or Sunday morning; last 4 weeks.
section: Sleep regularity
  sub: How often you are asleep — or awake — at the same time of day as you were the day before, over the last 30 days. 100 means identical sleep and wake times every day. Naps count.
section: Sleep and your next morning
  sub: Your morning HRV and recovery after nights with 7+ hours asleep (the adult recommendation, Watson et al. 2015) against shorter nights, across your whole history. "Clear" means 30+ mornings on each side and a gap too large to be chance.
